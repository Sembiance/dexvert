OUTCAST SOUND EFFECTS (SFX): MONO GSM FULL RATE PROFILE
=====================================================

1. Scope and confidence
-----------------------

This document specifies the Outcast SFX profile identified by the twelve-byte
prefix 32 54 76 98 01 00 00 00 00 00 80 3F. It describes the complete container,
every encoded audio bit, the fixed-point decoding procedure, and explicit
policies for damaged input. An SFX of this profile contains one mono stream.
The filename and extension are not part of identification.

There is one unresolved historical detail: the original engine-level meanings
of the constant words at offsets 4 and 8 are not established. They are often
described as a channel count and a floating-point compression factor, but those
names must not be treated as verified semantics. Their exact encodings and
required values are known and checked. Neither word needs interpretation to
decode this profile. Other values could indicate another profile, or damage;
there is no evidence-based rule for distinguishing those cases.

Consequently this is a complete byte layout and decoding specification for
the stated profile, not a claim to have proved the original purpose of every
header field or discovered all possible Outcast audio variants. Resolving that
remaining semantic uncertainty requires the original SFX loader/writer source
or its executable, followed by examination of the uses of offsets 4 and 8 and
controlled tests with changed values. Unknown variants must not be guessed.

2. Container layout
-------------------

All header integers are unsigned 32-bit little-endian. Offsets are absolute
byte offsets from the beginning of the file. There is no alignment padding.

 Offset  Bytes  Name used here       Encoding and meaning
 ------  -----  ------------------  -----------------------------------------
 0x00       4   signature           0x98765432; bytes 32 54 76 98.
 0x04       4   profile_word        Exactly 0x00000001 in this profile.
 0x08       4   factor_bits         Exactly 0x3F800000; these bits also encode
                                    IEEE-754 binary32 +1.0, little-endian.
 0x0C       4   data_bytes          Number D of compressed payload bytes.
 0x10       4   sample_rate         Playback samples per second, R.
 0x14       D   audio               Consecutive 33-byte GSM Full Rate frames.

The header is exactly 20 bytes. The total well-formed file length is 20 + D;
D must be divisible by 33. There is no checksum, footer, timestamp, index,
substream directory, loop marker, name, original uncompressed length, or
additional metadata chunk. Any bytes beyond 20 + D are extraneous data, not
another implicitly concatenated sound. No header integer is an offset.

For F = D / 33:

  frame i begins at 20 + 33*i, for 0 <= i < F;
  decoded sample count = 160*F;
  decoded 16-bit PCM byte count = 320*F;
  duration in seconds = 160*F/R;
  compressed byte rate = 33*R/160.

The playback rate is taken from the header. In particular, R = 22050 means
22050 Hz playback, not 8000 Hz. GSM's telephony convention of 8000 Hz does not
override the container's rate. Decoding produces sample values directly;
there is no resampling, rate estimation, volume normalization, or trimming.
The last frame is retained in full because no exact pre-encoding sample count
is stored. D = 0 can be represented as an empty stream.

3. Frame packing: every bit
--------------------------

Each frame occupies 33 bytes = 264 bits. It is the ordinary libgsm/toast
packing, NOT the Microsoft WAV49 packing of two frames into 65 bytes, and NOT
the reordered, error-protected GSM radio-channel representation.

Number the bits in a frame from 0 through 263. Bit 0 is the most significant
bit of byte 0; bit 7 is its least significant bit; bit 8 is the most significant
bit of byte 1, and so on. All fields below are unsigned binary integers, most
significant bit first. Byte boundaries do not interrupt a field.

 Frame bits  Width  Field       Meaning
 ----------  -----  ----------  --------------------------------------------
   0..3         4   magic       Exactly binary 1101 (hexadecimal D).
   4..9         6   LARc[0]     Coded log-area ratio 0, range 0..63.
  10..15        6   LARc[1]     Coded log-area ratio 1, range 0..63.
  16..20        5   LARc[2]     Coded log-area ratio 2, range 0..31.
  21..25        5   LARc[3]     Coded log-area ratio 3, range 0..31.
  26..29        4   LARc[4]     Coded log-area ratio 4, range 0..15.
  30..33        4   LARc[5]     Coded log-area ratio 5, range 0..15.
  34..36        3   LARc[6]     Coded log-area ratio 6, range 0..7.
  37..39        3   LARc[7]     Coded log-area ratio 7, range 0..7.
  40..95       56   subframe 0  First 40 reconstructed residual samples.
  96..151      56   subframe 1  Next 40 reconstructed residual samples.
 152..207      56   subframe 2  Next 40 reconstructed residual samples.
 208..263      56   subframe 3  Last 40 reconstructed residual samples.

For subframe j in 0..3 let B = 40 + 56*j. Its fields are:

 Frame bits       Width  Field       Meaning
 ---------------  -----  ----------  ---------------------------------------
 B+0 .. B+6          7   Nc          Long-term prediction lag, in samples.
 B+7 .. B+8          2   bc          Long-term prediction gain table index.
 B+9 .. B+10         2   Mc          Excitation grid offset, 0..3.
 B+11.. B+16         6   xmaxc       Quantized excitation amplitude, 0..63.
 B+17.. B+55        39   xmc[0..12]  Thirteen unsigned three-bit pulse codes.

More precisely, pulse xmc[k], for k in 0..12, occupies bits
B+17+3*k through B+19+3*k. Its range is 0..7. The last pulse ends at B+55.

There are 76 codec parameters: 8 LAR codes plus 4*(4 + 13) subframe values.
The bit total is 4 + 36 + 4*(7 + 2 + 2 + 6 + 13*3) = 264. There are no unused
bits, implicit pad bits, or opaque audio bytes. The signature nibble is a
checked constant, not a pulse parameter. All 260 remaining bits affect codec
parameters. Repacking these values in the listed order reproduces the frame.

Valid encoded pitch lags are 40..120. The seven-bit field can also represent
0..39 or 121..127; GSM's decoder rule in that case is to reuse the previous
valid lag, initially 40. Every representable value of the other fields is
defined by the decoder. There is no frame checksum with which to detect a
changed but still valid parameter.

4. Exact fixed-point synthesis
-----------------------------

The following defines the decoding operations without requiring another
implementation. All array indices are zero-based. Intermediate expressions
use unbounded signed integers except at the explicit saturation points.
Right shifts are arithmetic: negative results round toward negative infinity.

  S(x)   = max(-32768, min(32767, x))
  M(a,b) = S((a*b + 16384) >> 15)

M is rounded Q15 multiplication, including saturation of (-32768)*(-32768).
Do not replace integer shifts with floating-point arithmetic or truncation
toward zero. Ordering of saturation and rounding operations matters.

Initialize these persistent decoder values once at the start of the stream:

  history[0..119] = 0  (the last 120 reconstructed residual samples)
  previous_lar[0..7] = 0
  v[0..8] = 0         (short-term synthesis filter memory)
  previous_lag = 40
  deemphasis = 0

Do not reset this state at each frame. Parameters can be unpacked independently,
but bit-exact sound synthesis depends on earlier frames.

4.1. Excitation reconstruction, for each of four subframes

Let a = xmaxc. Compute exponent e and mantissa m:

  e = (a >> 3) - 1 if a > 15, otherwise 0
  m = a - (e << 3)
  if m == 0:
      e = -4
      m = 7
  otherwise:
      while m <= 7:
          m = (m << 1) | 1
          e = e - 1
      m = m - 8

Now e is in -4..6 and m is in 0..7. Use this direct mantissa table:

  FAC = [18431, 20479, 22527, 24575, 26623, 28671, 30719, 32767]

Let t = 6 - e. Set rounding = 2^(t-1) when t > 0, otherwise zero.
Start excitation[0..39] at zero. For k = 0..12:

  q = M(FAC[m], (2*xmc[k] - 7) << 12)
  excitation[Mc + 3*k] = S(q + rounding) >> t

Only those thirteen grid positions receive excitation pulses. In particular,
Mc = 3 is meaningful and places the last pulse at index 39.

4.2. Long-term synthesis, for each subframe in sequence

Set N = Nc if 40 <= Nc <= 120, otherwise N = previous_lag.
Set previous_lag = N. The Q15 gain table is:

  gain = [3277, 11469, 21299, 32767][bc]

For k = 0..39, using the history at the start of this subframe:

  d[k] = S(excitation[k] + M(gain, history[120 + k - N]))

Replace history with history[40..119] followed by d[0..39]. Append d to the
frame's residual array w. After four subframes, w contains 160 values.

4.3. Decoding the log-area ratios

The following tables are indexed by i = 0..7:

  MIC  = [-32, -32, -16, -16, -8, -8, -4, -4]
  B2   = [0, 0, 4096, -5120, 188, -3584, -682, -2288]
  INVA = [13107, 13107, 13107, 13107, 19223, 17476, 31454, 29708]

For each i:

  q = S(((LARc[i] + MIC[i]) << 10) - B2[i])
  q = M(INVA[i], q)
  current_lar[i] = S(q + q)

4.4. Interpolating coefficients and converting to reflection coefficients

There are four short-term interpolation regions within a frame. Their sizes
are 13, 14, 13, and 120; these are distinct from the 40-sample excitation
subframes. For each coefficient let p = previous_lar[i], c = current_lar[i].

 Residual sample indices  Interpolated log-area ratio L
 -----------------------  -------------------------------------------------
   0..12                  S(S((p >> 2) + (c >> 2)) + (p >> 1))
  13..26                  S((p >> 1) + (c >> 1))
  27..39                  S(S((p >> 2) + (c >> 2)) + (c >> 1))
  40..159                 c

For each L, let z = min(abs(L), 32767). Convert to a reflection magnitude:

  if z < 11059:       r = z << 1
  else if z < 20070:  r = z + 11059
  else:              r = S((z >> 2) + 26112)

The signed coefficient is -r if L < 0, otherwise r. This gives rrp[0..7]
for the region. After deriving the regions, retain current_lar as the next
frame's previous_lar.

4.5. Short-term synthesis and postprocessing

For each residual sample in order, select its region's rrp and set s = w[k].
For i descending from 7 to 0:

  s = S(s - M(rrp[i], v[i]))
  v[i+1] = S(v[i] + M(rrp[i], s))

Then:

  v[0] = s
  deemphasis = S(s + M(deemphasis, 28180))
  output_sample = S(deemphasis + deemphasis) & ~7

The last expression clears the three least significant bits in the signed
16-bit sample, leaving a value in -32768..32760. Write the sample as a signed
16-bit little-endian PCM integer. Retain v, history, previous_lar,
previous_lag, and deemphasis for the next frame. Exactly 160 samples result
from every complete frame.

5. Recognition and damage handling
---------------------------------

These rules distinguish the format definition from the converter's explicit
recovery policy. Damaged bytes cannot in general be restored exactly. There
is no checksum, redundancy, or original length field to make that possible.

Recognition:
  Read and validate the 20-byte header before reading the payload. Require
  the exact signature and the supported words at offsets 4 and 8. Do not
  identify by extension, search for signatures at arbitrary positions, guess
  an alternate codec, or attempt another format. Unsupported prefixes create
  no output file or directory, including when -verbose is supplied.

Unavailable header fields:
  A short header cannot establish both payload size and playback rate. A zero
  playback rate is unusable. These cases produce a diagnostic and no output.
  No substitute rate is guessed. RIFF PCM also requires R <= 0x7FFFFFFF so its
  unsigned 32-bit byte-rate field can hold 2*R.

Truncated payload:
  Use the smaller of D and the number of physically available payload bytes.
  Decode its complete 33-byte frame slots. Report missing bytes. A partial
  final frame cannot produce correct audio, so report and discard it. Never
  pad a compressed frame with invented values or synthesize frames for an
  absent tail.

Incorrect alignment of D:
  Report D modulo 33 != 0. Complete frames before that boundary remain usable;
  the incomplete remainder is accounted for but not decoded.

Extra physical bytes:
  Bytes beyond the declared payload boundary are reported and excluded. Do
  not reinterpret them as additional audio or concatenate a second stream.
  This honors the explicit length instead of guessing that the length is
  wrong. A -verbose audit records the exact extra bytes in hexadecimal.

Damaged frame signature after format recognition:
  A complete slot whose high nibble is not D is reported. Substitute 160 zero
  PCM samples to retain its time position and reset decoder state before
  continuing at the next fixed 33-byte slot. Do not scan for a new boundary.
  This is a documented loss-concealment policy, not recovered original sound
  and not a claim that silent samples were encoded in the damaged frame.

Out-of-range pitch lag:
  Apply the codec-defined previous-lag rule and report the occurrence. Do
  not reset the stream or discard the other parameters of that subframe.

No recoverable frames:
  A nonempty/damaged stream with no complete frame bearing a valid signature
  produces no output. A well-formed empty stream produces an empty PCM WAV.

Limits of detection:
  Altered parameter bits or a positive but incorrect playback rate cannot be
  detected without external evidence. Deleted or inserted bytes can destroy
  framing; boundary scanning is intentionally not used. Following a reset,
  later audio can differ from the undamaged original until predictive state
  recovers. Recovery messages go to standard error even without -verbose.

6. WAV representation and converter interface
---------------------------------------------

Command:

  python3 outcastSoundEffects.py <inputFile> <outputDir>

The converter creates outputDir if needed and accepts an existing directory.
It writes one file, <input filename without final extension>.wav. An existing
file with that output name is replaced only after decoding finishes. Paths
that alias the input itself are rejected. Other files in outputDir are left
alone. No metadata, extracted compressed stream, report, or sidecar is retained
by default. A transient staging file is used for atomic publication and is
removed on ordinary write failure. Files have mode 0644; newly created
directories permit both user and group to read and traverse them.

With -verbose (or --verbose), the converter additionally writes a .json file
with header values, a complete byte-region accounting, all frame parameters,
damaged/trailing bytes, and warnings, and prints a conversion summary.

--decoder auto    Use installed libgsm when available; otherwise use Python.
--decoder python  Use the embedded fixed-point decoder only.
--decoder libgsm  Require the external libgsm shared library explicitly.

Python 3.9 or newer and its standard library are sufficient. No supporting
data files, compiler, package installation, FFmpeg, or network access are
required. The optional libgsm accelerator must export gsm_create, gsm_decode,
and gsm_destroy with their normal C API. It uses ordinary 33-byte packing and
maintains one codec state per continuous stream. Both backends implement the
same synthesis procedure. The source contains libgsm's permission notice.

Exit status is zero when a WAV is written, including documented recoveries.
An unsupported/unrecoverable input, dependency error for explicitly requested
libgsm, or I/O error gives a nonzero exit status and a standard-error message.
Input validation and decoding precede creation of any output directory.
An I/O failure during optional JSON writing can leave the completed WAV.

Output WAV has the following canonical layout, all integers little-endian.
Let P = number of PCM samples * 2.

 Offset  Bytes  Value
 ------  -----  -----------------------------------------------------------
  0         4   ASCII RIFF
  4         4   36 + P
  8         4   ASCII WAVE
 12         4   ASCII fmt followed by one space
 16         4   16 (format chunk size)
 20         2   1 (integer PCM)
 22         2   1 (one channel)
 24         4   R (sample rate)
 28         4   2*R (byte rate)
 32         2   2 (block alignment)
 34         2   16 (bits per sample)
 36         4   ASCII data
 40         4   P
 44         P   Signed 16-bit little-endian PCM

P is even, so no data-chunk padding is needed. There are no ancillary chunks.
The total size is 44 + P. Inputs expanding beyond the RIFF 32-bit size limit
(36 + P > 0xFFFFFFFF) are rejected before output rather than silently wrapped.
The implementation holds compressed input and decoded PCM in memory; available
memory can impose a lower practical limit. This profile has only one stream,
so splitting a valid input into multiple WAVs is unnecessary.

7. Sources and verification basis
---------------------------------

The container prefix, byte counts, frame boundaries, and rate interpretation
are independently described in James McNeill's SFX converter:
  https://github.com/mcneja/sfx2wav/blob/master/sfx2wav.cpp
  https://playtechs.blogspot.com/2010/05/reading-outcast-sfx-files.html

The codec is GSM 06.10 RPE-LTP full-rate speech transcoding. Its normative
fixed-point procedures and tables are specified by ETSI:
  https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/100900_100999/100961/05.03.00_60/ts_100961v050300p.pdf

The conventional 33-byte packing, state initialization, and reference integer
implementation are available from the libgsm authors:
  https://www.quut.com/gsm/
  https://www.quut.com/gsm/gsm-1.0.23.tar.gz

The header's two constant words are specified here conservatively as profile
requirements. The converter does not turn unverified field names into codec,
channel-layout, resampling, or gain decisions. The next step for broader
historical format coverage is the loader/writer investigation in section 1.
