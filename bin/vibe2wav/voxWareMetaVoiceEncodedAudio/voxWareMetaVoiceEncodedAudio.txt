VOXWARE LEGACY VOX!/VOXd AUDIO
Binary container and compressed-frame reference
Revision 1, 2026-10-27

STATUS AND SCOPE

This is a verified description of the little-endian RIFF/WAVE container with
WAVE format tag 0x0062, a 40-byte VOX! descriptor, and a VOXd compressed stream.
It covers codec identifiers 0x0403, 0x0504, and 0x0605 and concatenations of
independent containers. A .vox extension alone does not identify this format.
Dialogic ADPCM, MagicaVoxel, other WAVE codecs, the later VOXh/VOX. wrappers,
and other Voxware codec identifiers are outside this document's implemented
scope. The term MetaVoice is sometimes used loosely for this entire family;
these three identifiers are distinct codec profiles.

THIS IS NOT A COMPLETE INDEPENDENT CODEC SPECIFICATION. Container fields,
frame packing and frame lengths have been recovered, but the full numerical
codebooks and synthesis algorithms have not been independently reconstructed.
The supplied converter delegates synthesis to original Voxware binaries.
Two bookkeeping fields and the intended interpretation of some encoder
metadata remain unresolved. Their positions and decoder behavior are stated
explicitly below. A byte coverage audit does not prove semantic understanding
of those bytes. A decoder cannot be reimplemented from this document alone.

1. NOTATION AND RECOGNITION

u16 and u32 mean unsigned little-endian 16-bit and 32-bit integers. f32 means
little-endian IEEE-754 binary32. Four-character identifiers are literal ASCII
bytes, case sensitive. All sizes in chunk headers are byte counts excluding
the eight-byte chunk header. All offsets in a table are relative to the start
identified immediately before that table.

A supported stream must satisfy all of the following structural conditions:
  * Begin with RIFF and the WAVE form type, at the declared container boundary.
  * Contain a fmt chunk with tag 0x0062, one channel, and a supported PCM
    description. Its data chunk must begin with VOX!, with descriptor size 40.
  * Have one VOXd chunk and one supported codec descriptor; the codec profile
    and the two sample rates must agree.
Do not search arbitrary byte strings for RIFF, VOX!, or VOXd to find streams.
These sequences can occur in compressed audio and are not resynchronization
markers. File extensions and guessed durations are not recognition tests.

2. RIFF FORM

Offset  Size  Type   Meaning
0       4     ASCII  "RIFF"
4       4     u32    Form length: all bytes after this field through form end
8       4     ASCII  "WAVE"
12      ...         Consecutive RIFF chunks

The form's logical end is start + 8 + length. RIFF chunks are padded to an
even byte boundary. A chunk of odd length has one alignment byte, excluded
from its chunk length. This byte is not audio; its value is not interpreted.
The converter accepts a missing final alignment byte at EOF as recoverable
damage and warns. A form of odd length is itself aligned before a following
form; its alignment byte is outside its own length.

Chunk layout:
  +0  four-byte chunk identifier
  +4  u32 payload length
  +8  payload
  +8+length  one alignment byte when length is odd

The ordinary sequence is fmt , fact, data. Container walking is length driven,
so recognized RIFF chunks need not have fixed absolute offsets. Other chunk
types are skipped by their lengths and reported as uninterpreted metadata.
Their internal semantics are not claimed to be implemented. Multiple fmt or
data chunks in one form, and extended fmt descriptors, are not implemented.

3. fmt CHUNK

The identifier is "fmt " including its final space; payload length is 16.

Offset  Size  Type  Meaning
0       2     u16   wFormatTag = 0x0062 (legacy Voxware)
2       2     u16   nChannels = 1
4       4     u32   nSamplesPerSec: 8000, 11025, or 22050
8       4     u32   nAvgBytesPerSec: declared average compressed byte rate
12      2     u16   nBlockAlign: original PCM sample width, 1 or 2
14      2     u16   wBitsPerSample: original PCM precision, 8 or 16

The last two fields do not describe compressed frame sizes. Compressed frames
are bit packed and need not end at byte boundaries. The average byte rate is
informational and may reflect encoder calculations rather than an exact
payload-size/duration quotient. It must not be used to find frame boundaries.
There is no cbSize field in the supported 16-byte descriptor.

4. fact CHUNK

The identifier is "fact"; the usual payload length is four bytes:
  +0 u32 declared uncompressed length/count

Ordinary WAVE specifies an uncompressed sample count here. Legacy Voxware
writers are inconsistent: the value can reflect a PCM byte count, an original
sample count, or a stale count inconsistent with the encoded frames. The exact
writer-dependent rules have not been recovered. There is no reliable flag
that selects one interpretation. Consequently this converter preserves and
reports the value but does not infer its units, trim decoded audio to it, or
pad audio to reach it. Output length comes from actual codec decoding.
An absent or short fact chunk does not prevent recovery of complete frames.

5. data CHUNK AND NESTED VOXWARE CHUNKS

The RIFF "data" payload contains a nested sequence beginning:
  "VOX!", u32(40), 40 descriptor bytes
  "VOXd", u32(compressed length), compressed bytes, optional alignment byte

The descriptor and VOXd header therefore occupy 56 bytes before the actual
compressed audio. In the conventional fmt/fact/data sequence, descriptor
payload starts at absolute offset 64 and compressed audio starts at 112.
Those absolute offsets are examples of layout arithmetic, not parser rules.

VOXd length is authoritative when it fits within its parent. When it exceeds
the enclosing data payload, the converter clamps it to the parent boundary
and reports the discrepancy. Very large lengths such as 0xffffff90 are
malformed unsigned lengths, not a newly invented negative-length convention.
The intact outer boundary still permits recovery. If the outer form is also
truncated, the physical EOF supplies the last available bound.

6. THE 40-BYTE VOX! DESCRIPTOR

Offsets below begin AFTER the VOX! chunk's eight-byte header.

Offset  Size  Type  Meaning and confidence
0       2     u16   Codec identifier (one combined word, not two versions)
2       2     u16   Encoder's nominal PCM samples-per-frame hint
4       2     u16   Original PCM bytes per sample, 1 or 2
6       2     u16   Encoder buffer bookkeeping; ignored by the decoder reader
8       4     u32   Packing flags: 0 for file mode; 6 for supported RT24
12      4     u32   Declared compressed frame count
16      4     u32   Encoder-private word; ignored by the decoder reader
20      4     u32   Nominal sample rate in Hz
24      4     f32   Low-pitch metadata (Hz; exact measurement convention unresolved)
28      4     f32   Mean-pitch metadata (Hz; exact measurement convention unresolved)
32      4     f32   High-pitch metadata (Hz; exact measurement convention unresolved)
36      4     f32   Stored amplitude scaling metadata

The sample-count hint can be rounded, can differ between writer versions,
and is not sufficient to predict exact decoded length. RT24 synthesis uses
180 samples per frame at 8000 Hz. File-mode synthesis has a nominal 15 ms
frame; this is 165.375 or 330.75 samples at the corresponding rates, so exact
integer output lengths depend on the reference synthesis timing/rounding.

The writer copies offset 6 from an internal output-buffer field. The writer
copies offset 16 from a process global. Their intended persistent semantics
have not been established; they must not be described as zero-filled padding,
a checksum, a timestamp, or an authorization key without further evidence.
The reference input reader does not use either value to reconstruct audio.

Offset 8 is generated by ORing bit 1 and bit 2 when the encoder's corresponding
packing-mode switch is set. Thus 6 means those two bits set, not six-byte
frames. Other combinations are unsupported by this implementation.

Pitch metadata is copied into codec state and can support voice alteration.
The bridge requests neutral pitch and speed. Its file-mode decoder initializes
the amplitude multiplier to 1 under its default API settings; the stored
scaling field is retained but the converter does not independently impose
gain normalization. Other player versions can produce different PCM.
Nonfinite metadata floats are reported and replaced only in the in-memory
decoder input, using zero for pitch fields and one for amplitude scaling.

7. CODEC IDENTIFIERS

Identifier  Descriptor bytes  Nominal rate  Profile
0x0403      03 04             11025 Hz      File-mode narrowband
0x0504      04 05              8000 Hz      RT24, 2400-bit/s speech
0x0605      05 06             22050 Hz      File-mode wideband

All three supported profiles are mono. Output is always 16-bit signed PCM,
including when original PCM precision was eight bits. Codec selection uses
the descriptor identifier, never the filename or estimated bit rate.

8. BIT ORDER AND RT24 FRAMES

Read each byte least significant bit first. The first bit read from a field
is its least significant bit. Frames follow immediately, with no per-frame
byte alignment, length word, synchronization word, or checksum.

An RT24 frame is exactly 54 bits:
  4 bits   voicing parameter
  8 bits   pitch parameter
  6 bits   energy parameter
  3 bits   spectral coefficient 0
  4 bits   spectral coefficient 1
  4 bits   spectral coefficient 2
  4 bits   spectral coefficient 3
  4 bits   spectral coefficient 4
  4 bits   spectral coefficient 5
  3 bits   spectral coefficient 6
  3 bits   spectral coefficient 7
  3 bits   spectral coefficient 8
  3 bits   spectral coefficient 9
  1 bit    additional synthesis parameter

The width vector is [4,8,6,3,4,4,4,4,4,3,3,3,3,1]. Each complete frame produces
180 PCM samples. The numerical mappings for the spectral values, the last
bit, and the complete RT24 synthesis have not been independently recovered.
The labels above describe decoder roles; they are not a substitute for the
missing codebooks and mathematical reconstruction rules.

9. FILE-MODE FRAMES

Each frame starts with V, a four-bit voicing index, followed by P, an eight-bit
pitch index. These select N, the number of following four-bit harmonic
amplitude values. N is zero for V=0. For V=1..15, use the exact table in
Appendix A. The reference parser caps the underlying table entries at 31.
No interpolation or guessed pitch formula is used to determine lengths.

Narrowband 0x0403 fields, in order:
  V: 4 bits
  P: 8 bits
  unvoiced-band energy index: 8 bits
  six spectral codebook indices: 5 bits each
  voiced energy index: 7 bits
  N harmonic amplitude indices: 4 bits each
Total: 57 + 4*N bits.

Wideband 0x0605 fields, in order:
  V: 4 bits
  P: 8 bits
  low-band unvoiced energy index: 8 bits
  six low-band spectral codebook indices: 5 bits each
  high-band energy index: 6 bits
  four high-band spectral codebook indices: 5 bits each
  voiced energy index: 7 bits
  N harmonic amplitude indices: 4 bits each
Total: 83 + 4*N bits.

The voicing value is V/15. The pitch index is mapped through a period ranging
from approximately 125 down to 16 internal samples, using a linear index
mapping; the converter deliberately uses the original table for frame length
instead of recomputing it from floating-point pitch. The spectral and energy
indices select nonlinear numerical tables. Synthesis combines harmonic and
noise components, spectral reconstruction, filtering, interpolation and,
for wideband, a second band. The exact tables, filter state equations, phase
generation, rounding and initialization rules remain a dependency on the
reference decoder. Describing the bit widths does not implement these steps.

10. END OF STREAM AND DAMAGE RECOVERY

Read at most the declared frame count, and never past VOXd's bounded payload.
If too few bits remain for a complete frame, do not send that partial frame
to synthesis. Reduce the frame count in the private decoder copy to the
number of complete frames. Do not fabricate padding frames or append silence.

The last encoded frame can end partway through a byte. Remaining bits in that
byte and any terminal fill byte are excluded from synthesis. They are distinct
from an outer RIFF alignment byte. Terminal bit values are not required to be
zero by this implementation; the decoder ignores them. The exact writer rule
for emitting an entire extra fill byte is not established. Verbose diagnostics
report the terminal bit count and raw terminal bytes, including the final
partially consumed byte. More than eight terminal bits are flagged.

The following operations are verified recovery, not searches or guesses:
  * Clamp a chunk's impossible length to its known enclosing boundary.
  * Clamp a truncated RIFF form to EOF.
  * Recover only complete frames using the exact field-width rules.
  * Tolerate a missing final alignment byte.
  * Stop at an invalid next concatenation boundary and report trailing bytes.

Unknown codec identifiers, other WAVE tags, unsupported packing flags,
unsupported channels/rates/precision, extended fmt layouts and alternative
Voxware wrappers are rejected. Missing structural identifiers cannot be
recovered by assuming that arbitrary data is compressed speech. The converter
does not attempt packet-loss concealment, bit-flip correction, or magic-byte
resynchronization. The format has no verified integrity checksum in this
supported layout, so complete-size frames can still contain damaged values.

11. CONCATENATED STREAMS

A file may consist of multiple independent RIFF/WAVE forms, with no archive
header, count, name table, or separator. Advance to the previous form's
declared end and optional even-alignment byte; parse the next form there.
Reset the codec stream state for each member. Do not decode concatenated
payloads as one continuous speech stream. Each form yields a separate WAV.
Member names are generated from the input stem and one-based source order.
There are no embedded pronunciation labels in this container structure.

12. CONVERTER INTERFACE AND OUTPUT

Usage:
  python3 voxWareMetaVoiceEncodedAudio.py <inputFile> <outputDir>
  python3 voxWareMetaVoiceEncodedAudio.py -verbose <inputFile> <outputDir>

outputDir may already exist. A single stream produces <input-stem>.wav.
Concatenations produce <input-stem>_000001.wav, _000002.wav, and so on. Six
digits are a minimum width, not a maximum member count. Existing matching
output filenames are refused rather than overwritten; unrelated files in
outputDir are allowed. Input files are never modified.

Default persistent conversion output consists only of WAV files. The command
prints status and warnings to the terminal. -verbose additionally writes
<input-stem>.voxware.json with offsets, descriptors, byte-range coverage,
frame counts, terminal bytes, output lengths and warnings. The listening HTML
is a separate one-time report, not a converter side effect. Newly created
output directories have mode 0775 and output files mode 0664 on POSIX.

Every supported RIFF member is structurally inspected before output begins.
A non-target codec in this prescan rejects the input before outputDir is
created. Recognized damaged streams can yield partial recovered audio. A
member that cannot be decoded has no WAV, while independently decodable
members can still be retained. Exit status 0 means WAV generation succeeded,
possibly with recovery warnings; 1 means a fatal error; 2 means some members
were skipped after others were decoded.

The bridge accepts at most 64 MiB of compressed input per member and allocates
at most 512 MiB of PCM per member. Oversized members are explicitly unsupported
by this implementation. No claim is made that these are format limits.

13. RUNTIME DEPENDENCIES AND IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

The Python module itself uses only the Python standard library (Python 3.10+).
It requires the adjacent voxware_support directory containing:
  decoder_bridge.exe  -- supplied 32-bit Windows IPC helper
  mm32dcmp.dll        -- original file-mode decoder
  VDK32116.DLL        -- original RT24 decoder
decoder_bridge.c supplies the helper's source. Rebuilding it uses an x86 MinGW
C compiler, for example:
  i686-w64-mingw32-gcc -O2 -s -static-libgcc -o decoder_bridge.exe decoder_bridge.c
The Python module pins binary SHA-256 hashes; a rebuild requires updating its
expected helper hash. Binaries are loaded by explicit path, without codec
registration or Windows system-directory installation.

On POSIX, Wine with 32-bit support and wineserver are required. With no explicit
WINEPREFIX, the converter creates an isolated temporary Wine prefix outside
outputDir, then shuts down that prefix and deletes it. Wine's temporary runtime
files are not conversion artifacts. An explicitly supplied WINEPREFIX is
reused; its lifecycle belongs to the caller. The bridge runs without a display.
Windows can run the helper directly. Linux/Wine is the validated environment;
the native Windows path has not received equivalent testing.

The helper receives bounded, reconstructed RIFF members over stdin and returns
raw PCM over stdout. It does not write intermediate audio files. File-mode
calls use a 90-byte ToolVox parameter block; RT24 uses a 142-byte block. The
input selector is 0x00010201 (compressed WAVE memory), and the output selector
is 0x00020200 (raw PCM memory). Output width is two bytes, neutral pitch is
requested, and speed is 1.0. Returned PCM byte count determines WAV length.
No use is made of FFmpeg's unrelated MetaSound decoder.

Voxware's synthesis can vary between decoder revisions and can include
pseudorandom noise components. The output is original-codec reconstruction,
not recovery of the pre-compression waveform and not a claim of bit-exact
agreement with every historical player.

14. EVIDENCE AND NEXT STEPS

The layout was checked against disassembly and controlled calls to the
original codecs. Useful MM32DCMP image addresses (base 0x10000000):
  0x100022a6: RIFF fmt/fact/data reader
  0x10002fc0: VOX!/VOXd reader
  0x10002d98: VOX! writer, including private/bookkeeping fields
  0x100014d1: LSB-first variable-length frame reader
  0x10012a58: narrowband field-width initialization
  0x10012935: wideband field-width initialization
  0x10011532: harmonic count lookup and cap
  0x1000ed19, 0x1000ea6e: narrowband/wideband parameter reconstruction
  0x100159e6: amplitude handling and synthesis dispatch
  0x1005f710: harmonic-count table
VDK32116 address 0x10029830 initializes the 54-bit RT24 width vector; address
0x100201e0 reads the descriptor; 0x100134f0 sets RT24's 180-sample frame size.

Original binary provenance and hashes are recorded in voxware_support/NOTICE.txt.
The historic WAVE tag registry is RFC 2361:
https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2361.html
RIFF WAVE chunk background:
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/xaudio2/resource-interchange-file-format--riff-

To finish a self-contained implementation and specification:
  1. Recover and publish every spectral, energy, pitch, harmonic and filter
     table for the three profiles, with exact index-to-value mappings.
  2. Reconstruct the synthesis, filter state, interpolation, phase/noise state,
     initialization and final flushing, including x87 precision/rounding.
  3. Resolve the header bookkeeping and encoder-private values, pitch-statistic
     conventions, fact-writer inconsistencies, and terminal fill-byte rule by
     tracing original encoder implementations and controlled encodings.
  4. Build independent implementations and compare their PCM against reference
     decoders for exhaustive field boundaries, varied speech and corruption.
  5. Test other documented Voxware wrappers/profile combinations separately;
     do not silently equate them with this supported container.
