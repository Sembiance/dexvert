CRI USM / Sofdec Movie container
MPEG-1/Sofdec video, ADX audio, and SBT text subtitle profile
======================================================================

STATUS AND SCOPE

This is an independently researched description, not a CRI specification.
It describes a usable, precisely bounded profile of the CRI USM container,
including its older headers. It is NOT a complete specification of every
Sofdec2 codec or of every proprietary metadata field. In particular, the
seven-byte prefix of certain MPEG user-data records, the exact derivation
of several encoder buffer hints, and some subtitle header fields remain
unresolved. These limitations must not be confused with byte coverage:
identifying the extent of a field does not establish its full semantics.

The accompanying converter recognizes CRID/@UTF containers, decodes clear
MPEG-1 with the supported Sofdec DC extension, decodes unencrypted type-3
ADX version 3/4, and extracts SBT subtitles. MPEG decoding, inverse DCT,
FFV1 encoding, and AVI muxing use FFmpeg. The Python code implements the
container and ADX decoder itself; it does not depend on FFmpeg's USM reader.
The MPEG entropy codebooks are not reproduced here. ISO/IEC 11172-2 and
the referenced MPEG decoder define that part of the bitstream.

Encrypted streams, HCA, H.264, VP9, MPEG-2, alpha streams, additional stream
identifiers, and other ADX encodings are outside this implementation. They
are not silently treated as the supported codecs. With -verbose, subtitle
text is emitted to SRT sidecars; it is not embedded or burned into the AVI. AVI preserves
all decoded video and audio streams independently, not a mix of languages.

The converter normalizes video to a zero start and a constant declared
frame rate. General nonzero stream start delays, variable presentation
timelines, and proprietary display/color/alpha transforms are not fully
implemented. Nonuniform video packet clocks are reported. No language
mapping is inferred from subtitle channel numbers or source filenames.

INTEGER AND OFFSET CONVENTIONS

Unless specified otherwise, integers are unsigned big-endian. Positions
are byte offsets. A range [a,b) includes a and excludes b. Four-byte tags
are literal ASCII, not host-endian integers. Metadata types can explicitly
select signed integers or IEEE-754 floating point. SBT records are the
important little-endian exception. ADX PCM output is signed little-endian
16-bit, although ADX headers and scales are big-endian.

There is no independent file-size field before the first chunk, no RIFF
wrapper, and no MPEG program-stream or transport-stream wrapper. A USM is
a concatenation of length-delimited chunks. Elementary stream bytes must
be taken from content chunks only; UTF tables, control messages, and
declared transport padding are not codec input.

CHUNK HEADER

Every ordinary chunk begins with this 32-byte header:

 Offset  Bytes  Interpretation
 0x00      4    Chunk identifier: CRID, @SFV, @SFA, or @SBT in this profile.
 0x04      4    Body size B; excludes the initial eight bytes.
 0x08      1    Reserved/unknown flag byte; zero in the clear profile.
 0x09      1    Payload offset H, measured from chunk position + 8.
 0x0A      2    Trailing padding size P.
 0x0C      1    Stream channel number. Independent namespaces per tag.
 0x0D      2    Reserved; zero in the supported profile.
 0x0F      1    Payload kind, described below.
 0x10      4    Scheduling timestamp T.
 0x14      4    Scheduling clock rate R.
 0x18      8    Reserved; zero in the supported profile.

For a chunk at absolute position C:

 next chunk          = C + 8 + B
 payload start       = C + 8 + H
 payload end         = C + 8 + B - P
 payload byte count  = B - H - P
 extended header     = [C + 32, C + 8 + H)
 trailing padding    = [C + 8 + B - P, C + 8 + B)

Require B >= 24, H >= 24, H + P <= B, and all available ranges within the
file. H is commonly 24, making the payload begin immediately after the
32-byte header. P can be zero or quite large. Padding is zero-filled in
the supported profile. Readers must use the stored lengths, not assume
32-byte or 2048-byte alignment. Header chunks are often aligned to 32/64
bytes, the directory often occupies 2048 bytes, and data chunk alignment
can vary. None of those common sizes is a file-identification rule.

Payload kinds:

 0  Contents: video elementary bytes, audio elementary bytes, SBT records.
 1  Header: an @UTF directory or stream-information table.
 2  Control: one of the exact 32-byte messages below.
 3  Metadata: an @UTF table, such as a video seek index.

The following control strings include their spaces and one final zero:

 '#HEADER END     ===============\0'
 '#METADATA END   ===============\0'
 '#CONTENTS END   ===============\0'

Each is scoped by its enclosing tag and channel. There is no single global
end marker required after the last stream. Type-2 payloads are not media.
The first chunk is CRID kind 1 containing CRIUSF_DIR_STREAM. A common phase
order is directory, per-stream headers, header-end controls, seek metadata,
metadata-end controls, interleaved media chunks, content-end controls.
Readers can follow chunk sizes without assuming this precise ordering.

TIMING

T/R gives a nominal scheduling time in seconds where R is nonzero. Video
content commonly advances T by 100, with R equal to 100 times its frame
rate. The stream header supplies the rational playback frame rate as
framerate_n/framerate_d. Preserve this ratio exactly: a decimal 29.97
and the MPEG sequence-header rate 30000/1001 are different rationals.

Video packets are in decoding order. MPEG picture temporal_reference and
I/P/B dependencies determine display order; assigning each compressed
packet a display timestamp directly would mishandle B pictures. The
converter supplies the declared constant rate to the MPEG decoder and
lets it reorder pictures before AVI encoding.

ADX content includes an initial ADX header packet and terminal ADX marker.
Their timestamps do not represent audio samples. Audio packet scheduling
clocks need not precisely match accumulated ADX samples. Correct audio
duration is total_samples / sampling_rate, with each sample counted once
per channel. Do not stretch audio to the packet clock or video length.

UTF TABLE ENCODING

A kind-1 or kind-3 table payload starts with the following header:

 Offset  Bytes  Interpretation
 0x00      4    '@UTF'
 0x04      4    Table size S, excluding the first eight bytes.
 0x08      4    Row region offset, relative to table + 8.
 0x0C      4    String pool offset, relative to table + 8.
 0x10      4    Binary/blob region offset, relative to table + 8.
 0x14      4    Table name offset, relative to the string pool.
 0x18      2    Column count N.
 0x1A      2    Fixed row byte length L.
 0x1C      4    Row count M.
 0x20     ...   Column descriptors, followed by row and pool regions.

Validate the region order. The final logical table byte is at S + 8.
Each column descriptor consists of a one-byte flag and a four-byte name
offset into the string pool. A constant value follows its descriptor if
the storage class requires it. Names and ordinary strings end with NUL.
Strings are commonly UTF-8. The converter retains raw string bytes as
hexadecimal too, so non-UTF-8 names do not require an encoding guess.

The flag's high nibble is the storage class:

 0x1: zero/default value, with no encoded value in descriptor or rows.
 0x3: a single constant follows the descriptor and applies to all rows.
 0x5: a separate value is present in each row.

The flag's low nibble selects the type:

 0x0 / 0x1: unsigned / signed 8-bit integer, one byte.
 0x2 / 0x3: unsigned / signed 16-bit integer, two bytes.
 0x4 / 0x5: unsigned / signed 32-bit integer, four bytes.
 0x6 / 0x7: unsigned / signed 64-bit integer, eight bytes.
 0x8: IEEE-754 binary32, four bytes.
 0x9: IEEE-754 binary64, eight bytes.
 0xA: four-byte string-pool-relative offset.
 0xB: two four-byte integers: blob-relative offset and blob byte count.

For example, 0x54 means a per-row u32, 0x52 a per-row u16, 0x50 a per-row
u8, and 0x5A a per-row string pointer. Every row stores its per-row columns
in descriptor order. The sum of their encoded widths must be exactly L.
Row i begins at table + 8 + row_offset + i*L. A blob's bytes are not stored
inline in the row; its descriptor points into the binary region.

Null/default string values refer to offset zero. '<NULL>' is a common
ordinary string-pool sentinel, not a magic separate integer value. Strings
may share offsets. Readers should bounds-check every string terminator and
blob extent. Extra zero space between sections or at the end is alignment;
unreferenced nonzero bytes must be retained and reported as unresolved.
Encrypted UTF signatures and other storage classes are not implemented.

DIRECTORY TABLE: CRIUSF_DIR_STREAM

This directory commonly has one container row and one row per stream.
These are schema names, not fields at invariant row byte offsets:

 filename  String. Encoder/source label; may refer to an original source
           filename. It is not a path that a decoder should open.
 filesize  u32. Container row: physical USM byte length. Stream row: encoded
           elementary byte count. Video per-packet zero prefixes may be
           excluded. Includes ADX header and end marker for audio.
 datasize  u32. Additional encoder size field, often zero. Its nonzero
           semantics are not established by this implementation.
 stmid     u32. FourCC packed big-endian, e.g. 0x40534656 is @SFV,
           0x40534641 is @SFA, 0x40534254 is @SBT. Zero is the container row.
 chno      u16. Stream channel number; 0xFFFF denotes the container row.
 minchk    u16. Decoder buffering/chunk-count hint; does not size the file.
 minbuf    u32. Buffer-size hint in bytes. For the supported video profile
           it equals the largest individual encoded picture payload after
           excluding its zero prefix. Container/audio/subtitle formulas
           remain unresolved; do not apply the video rule to other tags.
 avbps     u32. Nominal average encoded bits per second. For video/audio,
           floor(8*filesize/playback_seconds). The container value is the
           sum of the stream values. Subtitle scheduling uses its cue span
           rather than the video duration. This field is not a clock.

Directory values should be cross-checked with parsed stream identifiers
and byte totals. A stale directory size does not justify reading beyond
physical EOF. A decoder can still recover correctly delimited media from
a confirmed USM with damaged directory hints.

VIDEO_HDRINFO

 width, height         Coded image dimensions, u32.
 mat_width, mat_height Material/storage dimension metadata, u32.
 disp_width,
 disp_height           Display dimension metadata, u32.
 scrn_width            Additional screen-width metadata, u32; exact
                       nonzero rendering semantics are unresolved.
 mpeg_dcprec           DC coefficient precision in bits, u8; 8 or 11 in
                       the supported implementation.
 mpeg_codec            Codec selector, u8. This profile implements 1,
                       MPEG-1 including the Sofdec DC extension.
 alpha_type            u32. Zero means no alpha in the supported profile.
 total_frames          Number of coded pictures, u32.
 framerate_n,
 framerate_d           Rational playback frames per second, u32/u32.
 metadata_count        Number of metadata table chunks for the stream.
 metadata_size         Total allocated metadata chunk bytes, including
                       chunk headers and padding, excluding the following
                       metadata-end control; not just UTF payload bytes.
 ixsize                Video input buffer allocation: in this profile it
                       equals the largest picture payload without its zero
                       prefix, also stored in the directory's minbuf.
 pre_padding           Optional u32 count of zero prefix bytes per video
                       payload. Older headers can omit it while retaining
                       zero prefixes. Absence alone is not used to choose
                       or remove a fixed number of bytes.

Metadata is schema-driven. Column order and constant/per-row storage may
vary. The converter records all supplied columns. It uses coded dimensions
and the rational rate; arbitrary material/display transforms remain outside
its implemented profile. MPEG syntax is cross-checked against dimensions.

VIDEO_SEEKINFO

Metadata tables of this name contain these columns:

 ofs_byte   u64 absolute USM chunk offset, pointing at @SFV, not its payload.
 ofs_frmid  signed i32 zero-based frame index in coded packet order.
 num_skip   signed i16 preroll/skip count associated with the seek point.
 resv       signed i16 reserved value, normally zero.

Seek entries identify access points associated with sequence/GOP starts.
Read all rows, not just the first. Verify offset targets and frame indices.
Linear decoding does not need the index. Nonzero num_skip and resv are
retained, but precise random-access preroll behavior is not implemented.

MPEG-1 / SOFDEC ELEMENTARY VIDEO

Concatenate kind-0 payloads belonging to a single @SFV channel. Keep the
sequence headers, GOP headers, picture headers, slice data, user data,
sequence-end marker and legal zero stuffing. Never concatenate channels.
The directory elementary filesize can exclude zero bytes before the first
MPEG start code in each payload; those zero prefixes are accounted for
separately. Zero-prefix recognition uses the MPEG start-code syntax, not
filenames, file sizes, a guessed codec, or a search for a next USM chunk.

MPEG start codes are byte-aligned 00 00 01 followed by one byte:

 00: picture header. Temporal reference, picture coding type, VBV delay,
     optional motion-vector fields, and extra information precede slices.
 01..AF: slice headers and entropy-coded macroblocks, as defined by MPEG-1.
 B2: user data, extending to the next start code.
 B3: sequence header.
 B7: sequence end.
 B8: group-of-pictures header and time code.

The first 64 bits after B3 have fields of widths:
 12 width; 12 height; 4 aspect ratio; 4 frame-rate code;
 18 bitrate value; 1 marker bit; 10 VBV buffer size; 1 constrained flag;
 1 load-intra-matrix flag; 1 next bit (matrix data if that flag is set).
After the first 63 bits, parse quantization-matrix flags and data as a
bitstream, not as byte-aligned fields. A present matrix has 64 eight-bit
entries in zigzag order. The non-intra matrix flag follows the intra
matrix when one is present.

Frame-rate codes 1..8 correspond to 24000/1001, 24, 25, 30000/1001, 30,
50, 60000/1001 and 60. MPEG bitrate units are 400 bits/s; VBV units are
16384 bits. The container's exact rational playback rate takes precedence
when supplied. Intra/P/B picture prediction, motion compensation,
quantization, VLC decoding and IDCT follow MPEG-1 with the extension below.

Sofdec/TMPG user data can contain this byte sequence:

 TMPGEXS\0 00000028 IDCPREC\0 00000008 00000003 TMPGEXE\0 00000000

The eight-character numbers are ASCII hexadecimal, not binary integers.
The opening region declares 0x28 bytes: the 24-byte IDCPREC record plus
the 16-byte closing record. IDCPREC has an eight-byte value payload of
'00000003'. The value 3 selects precision 8+3 = 11 bits. Decoder behavior
requires the DC predictor to start at 128 << 3 and intra quantizer entry
zero to be 1. FFmpeg has a TMPGEXS path implementing this behavior.

A seven-byte proprietary prefix followed by NUL can precede TMPGEXS.
Its exact internal meaning is unresolved. The converter records every
distinct user-data byte string and occurrence count, passes it unchanged
to FFmpeg, and does not invent field names for its unknown bytes.
Other TMPG records and precision modes require further implementation.
MPEG-2 B5 extensions are explicitly rejected by this converter.

AUDIO_HDRINFO

 sampling_rate  u32 samples per second per channel.
 total_samples  u32 decoded samples per channel, including a partial
                last ADX block only up to the declared sample count.
 num_channels   u8 channels within this one audio stream.
 ixsize         u32 buffer/index allocation hint, exact derivation unresolved.
 audio_codec    Optional codec selector. 2 denotes ADX. Older headers omit
                this field; validate the actual ADX header in that case.

Multiple @SFA channel numbers are independent audio tracks; they must not
be mistaken for left and right channels of a single ADX stream. Stereo is
expressed by num_channels and the ADX header. Tracks can have different
lengths, and short tracks should not truncate the rest of the movie.

ADX TYPE 3 HEADER AND BLOCKS

Concatenate all kind-0 @SFA payloads for one stream. Its ADX header is:

 Offset Bytes Meaning
 0x00     2   0x8000.
 0x02     2   Offset O; compressed data starts at O + 4.
 0x04     1   Encoding 3: fixed predictor computed from high-pass frequency.
 0x05     1   Block byte size, 18 in this profile.
 0x06     1   Residual width, 4 bits.
 0x07     1   Audio channel count.
 0x08     4   Sample rate Hz.
 0x0C     4   Total decoded samples per channel.
 0x10     2   High-pass cutoff Hz.
 0x12     1   ADX header version, 3 or 4 here.
 0x13     1   Flags/revision, zero here. Encrypted variants are unsupported.

Version 4 adds four reserved bytes at 0x14 and initial predictor history
from 0x18. For each channel, two signed big-endian int16 values are stored:
the preceding sample h1, then the sample before that h2. The history area
occupies max(channels,2)*4 bytes, so mono also has an extra four-byte slot.
Version 3 has no initial history and starts h1=h2=0.

When room is present before the copyright signature, a 24-byte loop area
follows the base/history fields: two u16 fields for sample alignment and
loop type, followed by five u32 values for loop enable/count, loop start
sample, loop start byte, loop end sample, loop end byte. The converter
records these and converts one finite playback pass. Exact behavior of
unusual loop flags and additional AINF/CINF application extensions is not
implemented. Remaining header bytes are retained verbatim in the audit.

The six bytes immediately preceding compressed data must be '(c)CRI'.
Header size is O+4, not O and not a fixed 288. Header padding is not audio.

For channel count C, each block group has 18*C bytes, one 18-byte block per
channel in channel order. Each block stores a big-endian u16 scale word
followed by 16 bytes, or 32 signed four-bit residuals. Consume high nibble
first. Nibbles 0..7 mean 0..7; nibbles 8..15 mean -8..-1.

The type-3 scale is stored_scale + 1. The fixed predictors are calculated
from sample rate Fs and cutoff Fh:

 a = sqrt(2) - cos(2*pi*Fh/Fs)
 b = sqrt(2) - 1
 c = (a - sqrt((a+b)*(a-b))) / b
 k1 = trunc(c * 8192)
 k2 = trunc(-c*c * 4096)

For a version-4 block, each decoded sample is:

 predicted = (k1*h1 + k2*h2) >> 12
 sample = clamp_signed_16(residual*(stored_scale+1) + predicted)
 h2 = h1
 h1 = sample

The shift is arithmetic, including negative values. Prediction uses the
previous clipped sample. Version-3 early-decoder behavior computes the two
products' arithmetic shifts separately, then adds them. Later proprietary
decoders can decode version-3 streams with the version-4 rounding rule;
bit-exact equivalence to every historical decoder is not claimed.

Decode all channel blocks and retain at most total_samples output samples
per channel. The final block's extra residuals are codec padding; they are
decoded/accounted for but must not lengthen the output. Preserve initial
history values even when nonzero. Plain FFmpeg ADX decoding is not used
here because its observed implementation differs on scale/history handling.

At a block-group boundary, 0x8001 is a terminal marker. Its next u16 is the
number of remaining marker payload bytes; common markers are 18 bytes
total with 14 zero bytes after the two words. This is not an ADPCM block
and contributes no samples. Verify its declared extent and preserve any
unexpected trailing data in diagnostics. A truncated stream can yield
all complete block groups before the damage without synthesizing missing
tail samples. A damaged individual channel block can use zero residuals
with an explicit warning while retaining predictor continuity.

SBT TEXT SUBTITLES

@SBT kind-1 metadata is SUBTITLE_HDRINFO:

 time_unit      u32 header/mux scheduling metadata; it is not necessarily
                the time unit of SBT records.
 total_time     u32. Exact semantics unresolved; do not use it as cue end.
 num_channels   u8 number of distinct subtitle language/channel IDs.
 content_xsize  u32 maximum stored text byte count, including terminators.
 ixsize         u32 buffer/index hint, often zero.

Kind-0 payloads contain length-delimited subtitle records. All five words
in the following header are LITTLE-ENDIAN:

 Offset Bytes Meaning
 0x00     4   Language/channel ID; it is not a standardized ISO language ID.
 0x04     4   Ticks per second U, strictly positive.
 0x08     4   Cue start in ticks.
 0x0C     4   Cue duration in ticks, NOT an absolute end time.
 0x10     4   Text byte count N, including terminal zero byte(s).
 0x14     N   UTF-8 text followed by terminal zero byte(s).

The next record begins at current + 20 + N. A payload can contain multiple
records. Start seconds = start/U; end seconds = (start+duration)/U. Preserve
all terminal zero counts in a byte audit, but remove them for display.
Multiple languages can have records with identical start times in any
order; group by record language ID, not outer @SBT channel alone. Invalid
UTF-8 is retained as raw hex and shown with replacement characters.

AVI OUTPUT, SIDECARS AND DEPENDENCIES

Run:
 python3 criwareSofdec2Movie.py inputFile outputFile

By default, only the AVI output file is written. To also write metadata and
subtitle sidecars, pass -verbose (or its alias --verbose):
 python3 criwareSofdec2Movie.py -verbose inputFile outputFile

outputFile is written as AVI regardless of its extension. Use '.avi' for
player compatibility. The parent directory must already exist. Python
3.10 or newer, ffmpeg, and ffprobe on PATH are required. There are no
third-party Python modules, network calls, key files, compiled helpers,
or other supporting runtime files. --ffmpeg and --ffprobe accept explicit
executable paths. FFmpeg needs its MPEG-1 decoder, FFV1 encoder, PCM/WAV
support, and AVI muxer. FFV1 is lossless relative to decoded frames;
integer IDCT/color behavior can vary among decoder implementations.

The AVI contains FFV1 level-3 video with slice CRCs and signed 16-bit PCM
audio, preserving each independent track. This favors fidelity over file
size. Short audio tracks remain short. Audio is not trimmed to the shortest
stream. The source is never modified. The finalized AVI replaces an
existing destination only after encoding and output validation succeed.

Only with -verbose, the converter also writes:
 outputFile.json: SHA-256, complete chunk extent ledger, table schemas and
 values, timing fields, byte totals, original names, controls, video syntax
 counts, user-data hex, ADX headers and block accounting, subtitle records,
 output probe results, warnings, and explicit research limitations.
 outputFile.sbtC.languageL.srt: subtitle sidecars, one per outer stream C
 and record language ID L. Numbers deliberately do not guess language names.

Without -verbose, subtitle data is parsed but no SRT or JSON files are
written. Existing sidecars from earlier runs are not removed.

All finalized outputs are readable by both owner and group. Temporary
elementary streams and PCM WAVs are cleaned up automatically. Artifact
installation uses atomic replacement per file, not a transaction spanning
the AVI and all sidecars. An operating-system error during the final group
of replacements can leave some completed sidecars installed.

RECOGNITION, DAMAGE AND UNSUPPORTED INPUTS

Recognition requires CRID at byte zero, coherent first-chunk framing,
@UTF at the declared payload location, and a structurally valid table named
CRIUSF_DIR_STREAM with directory columns. A filename extension or a lone
four-byte signature is insufficient. Recognition occurs before temporary
or destination files are created. Failed recognition leaves no artifacts
and does not alter an existing destination. Errors go to stderr, exit 2.

Once identification is established, truncated final payloads, stale byte
counts, bad seek targets, damaged later metadata, unexpected padding and
missing end controls are reported. Media recovery follows known chunk
boundaries. The parser never searches arbitrary bytes for a plausible next
USM header. If a chunk size destroys knowledge of the next boundary, only
the certain prefix is used. A damaged root too incomplete for positive
identification is rejected. This conservative rule can reject real but
severely damaged USM files; it does not claim to recognize them by guesswork.

Unsupported codecs or stream types fail explicitly before destination
installation. Malformed ADX headers with no safe decoder parameters and
video without a recoverable sequence header can also prevent conversion.
There is no universal repair guarantee. FFmpeg can conceal damaged MPEG
blocks; diagnostics and a recovered-picture count are retained in the
audit. Unparsed corrupt tails are counted as unparsed, not understood.

BYTE ACCOUNTING AND REMAINING WORK

The chunk ledger partitions the physical file into headers, any extended
headers, typed payloads and trailing padding. Metadata parsing additionally
accounts for descriptors, row values, strings, blobs and region gaps.
Video start-code counts and byte lengths cover its compressed elementary
stream, with zero prefixes recorded. The MPEG decoder processes compressed
picture data. ADX accounting covers header, channel groups, trimmed final
samples and end marker. SBT accounting includes its five fields, text and
terminal bytes. A zero remainder in the physical ledger is a structural
coverage result, NOT a proof of complete semantic understanding.

To close the remaining gaps:

1. Obtain a documented vendor decoder/encoder interface or perform
   controlled encoding experiments varying one setting at a time to
   determine the proprietary MPEG prefix, buffer hints, datasize,
   material/screen dimensions, and subtitle total_time.
2. Establish exact random-seek preroll, arbitrary start offsets and
   variable timeline behavior against an independent reference player.
3. Verify video color/display transforms and integer decoder output
   against the vendor player, especially for differing display dimensions.
4. Add explicit, tested implementations for additional USM codec selectors,
   alpha and encrypted variants, using supplied keys where required.
5. Validate uncommon ADX application extensions and historical rounding
   modes against the corresponding reference decoder versions.
